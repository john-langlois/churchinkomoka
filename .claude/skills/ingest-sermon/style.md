# Sermon article style

Edit this file to change how sermons are summarised. Claude reads it every time before writing an article.

## Task
Turn the raw sermon transcript (YouTube auto-captions) into a polished, readable article for the church website. It is also the source for the podcast episode description.

## Structure
- Start with one engaging opening paragraph, no heading, that introduces the main theme. **Keep it under about 300 characters**: the podcast feed uses the first 300 characters of the article as the episode description, so it must stand on its own and hook the listener (core question or theme, key scripture or topic, warm and inviting).
- Then 4 to 8 sections. Every section has a short `##` heading that reflects a shift in topic or a new major point, followed by one or more paragraphs.
- End with a `## Closing` or `## Reflection` section that captures the conclusion or call to action.
- One blank line between every paragraph and heading.
- Headings and paragraphs only. No bullet lists, no numbered lists, no tables.
- Use **bold** only for direct scripture references (for example **John 3:16**) and key theological phrases.
- Do not include the title as a heading. The page already shows it.

## Voice and content
- Warm, pastoral, accessible to everyone.
- Be faithful to what was said: keep the preacher's key points, scripture references, illustrations and stories. Do not add teaching, quotes, verses or claims that are not in the transcript. Do not change the meaning or the order of the argument.
- Remove filler ("um", "you know"), false starts, stutters and repeated phrases. Keep the preacher's conviction and spiritual depth.
- Do not use em dashes.
- Write in third person about the sermon ("Mike reminds us...") or in the preacher's own inclusive voice ("we"), whichever reads more naturally; stay consistent within one article.

## Accuracy checks (auto-captions make mistakes)
- Fix obvious caption errors in names, places and Bible book names. Check every scripture reference is a real book, chapter and verse range.
- If a reference or name is unclear, do not guess. Flag it to the user in the preview as "check: ...".
- Length: roughly 15 to 25 percent of the spoken length is typical; do not pad.

## Title
- Use a clean title: the sermon's own title, without the speaker's name or the date (the site shows those separately). Example: "What is your story?" rather than "What is your story? Mike Willer, November 8th, 2026".
